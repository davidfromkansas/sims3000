# Create and play a city challenge

The manual includes a Scenario Creator with goals, time limits and scripts. This first editor supports one to four distinct metric goals, a title and a deadline from 12 to 1200 months. Metrics include population, treasury, education, crime, air pollution, road condition, wellbeing and life expectancy. All goals must be met together at a monthly check before or on the deadline.

The current city becomes the starting point. Existing buildings, funds and simulation state remain; any prior scenario goals are replaced. Export before beginning to preserve the original sandbox, or export immediately afterward to share the playable challenge starting state. Import/export uses the existing city-file workflow. Custom challenges do not have a built-in restart snapshot; reimport the saved starting file to replay.

Schema 35 saves the definition and progress. Existing prepared challenges remain unchanged. Scheduled disaster authoring is implemented. The full scripting language, the original scenario catalog remain unfinished.

Feedback: create a two-goal challenge, export it, then play through a few months and inspect its progress. Are goal targets, deadline and starting-state export clear?

Browser verification covered creating a named challenge and rendering live progress. An update-loading issue was reproduced and fixed by applying a consistent release key to game-module imports, preventing cached old modules from mixing with the new editor.

Scheduled events: authors choose up to four distinct months before the deadline, a disaster type and map coordinates. Events run once, before optional random disasters. Fire and riot targets are selected nearest the chosen location; earthquakes use nearest land. Unsuitable events are explicitly recorded as skipped. Victory waits for all events to resolve and for emergency response to finish. Schema 36 saves definitions and aligned event progress; earlier custom challenges migrate without scheduled events. Timing is monthly and the calendar pauses during emergencies.

Conditional events: the manual’s crime-threshold riot example motivates metric predicates. Authors can choose any challenge metric, at-least or at-most comparison, and a threshold. Checks begin at the event month and continue monthly until it triggers or the challenge ends. An unmet conditional event is an avoidable threat, not a required victory objective; active emergencies still block victory. Unmet conditions cannot block later timed events. Schema 37 adds a nullable condition to event definitions; previous schedules retain unconditional timing.

Scenario text: optional briefing, success and failure messages support up to 1,500 characters each. Paragraph line breaks are retained and markup is escaped rather than executed. Briefings appear throughout the challenge; only the matching outcome message appears after win or loss. Schema 38 stores these fields with the custom definition, and prior definitions receive empty text with a default briefing.

Repeating events: authors can choose 1–12 occurrences per event and an interval measured from the previous occurrence. Conditions are rechecked for every occurrence; false conditions delay the next attempt. Due events are prioritized by their eligible month. Required timed series must fully resolve before victory; conditional series remain avoidable. Schema 39 stores resolved counts and last occurrence months. Existing events default to a single occurrence. Definitions must nominally fit before the challenge deadline; delays can still cause a timed challenge to miss its deadline. This covers a bounded repeat capability, not the manual’s complete command language.

Combined conditions: schema 40 supports one to four metric comparisons joined with AND (all) or OR (any). All selected comparisons are evaluated against the same monthly city state; blank rows are ignored, and no selected conditions means an unconditional timed event. Existing single-predicate saves remain valid. The editor and event report expose the chosen combination. This implements the manual’s Boolean condition concept (p. 188), with a flat bounded group rather than arbitrary nested scripts.

Infrastructure goals (schema 41): the manual’s structure and utility counts (p. 186) now have playable goals for operating airports, operating seaports, active bus stops, actual bus and rail/subway commuters, and occupied residential buildings without power/water. Facility counts use complete operating footprints, not zoned tiles; commuters require actual connected routes. Residential utility counts exclude vacant zones and rubble; combine with a population goal to prevent depopulation from satisfying a service-restoration challenge. These metrics also work in conditional disaster groups. This does not yet implement arbitrary structure-type or radius queries.

Business objectives (schema 47): monthly business income, placed casinos, and placed toxic-waste plants are available as objectives and event conditions. Permits alone count neither as buildings nor income. A zero-toxic-waste goal can be paired with population or treasury requirements; demolishing the facility satisfies that constraint while removing its stipend. This implements a bounded subset of the manual’s Business Deal Status / structure-count scenario commands.

Sustained goals (schema 49): authors choose a holding period from one month through the challenge deadline. All metrics and required-event completion must hold at each monthly check; failure resets the streak. Calendar pauses during emergencies. Progress is counted once per month, saved with the existing streak field, and shown in the goals list. Earlier definitions default to one monthly check. A completed streak on the deadline still wins.
