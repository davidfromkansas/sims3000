# Science Center and sustained education

The fourteenth reward is the Science Center. From 1999, 90 EQ and strong city approval earn a durable offer at the next monthly evaluation. There is no population gate. Players pay §75,000 to place its unique 5×5 building, and may rebuild after demolition or destruction.

The operating center supplies 375 civic jobs, improves nearby residential and commercial land values, and produces air pollution, water pollution and 8 garbage units/month. Its full footprint supplies road access for collection. It adds no teaching places, education bonus, residential capacity, direct aura or direct crime effect. Inspection explains these tradeoffs; the education report explains the reward's long-term learning requirement and the Reading campaign now describes its adult learning contribution.

The [Prima reward directory](https://www.scribd.com/doc/303581554/SimCity-3000-Unlimited-Prima-Guide), page 415, supplies the 1999 date, original EQ requirement 135, approval 100, cost, footprint, jobs and effects. Original EQ is normalized from 0–150 to 0–100, making the requirement 90. Approval uses the established −127…127 normalization. Land-value magnitudes are 10/10/0; their 10-tile radius and falloff are reconstruction calibration. Air/water weights 1500/2000 normalize to 15/20 over radii 10/5. Table 14-2 supplies garbage weight 800, normalized to 8/month. Zero upkeep and the shared operational gate are reconstruction choices.

The original procedural miniature has a copper-colored planetarium dome, glazed research wings and rooftop solar panels. Four rotations are rasterized through the actual game model path. Schema 144 migrates older cities with an unearned Science Center and rejects placing it in older schemas.

Focused checks cover date/EQ/approval boundaries, absence of a population gate, offer persistence, unique paid placement and rebuilding, 375 filled jobs with sufficient workers, lack of teaching/capacity effects, sector land values, pollution, waste, damage, radiation, exact save continuity, migration, actual inspector text and four-view geometry. All 300 regular regression suites passed, including after the routing change below.

## Ordinary city progression

The staged ordinary-city route starts at month 749 with 204,784 residents and 70.119 EQ. Enabling Reading and renewing aging utilities through paid construction sustained the city, but education reached only 86.904 after 480 months. Adult population growth required another library and museum. Residential rebuilding also introduced lower-education newcomers; preserving homes after observed density declines stabilized the population without editing simulation rules or granting the reward.

The city reached 90.009 EQ naturally at month 1421, with 204,544 residents, 99.361 approval, full education coverage, zero garbage backlog and §40,884,461. Its Science Center offer was earned that month. Paid placement at (85,91), followed by twelve ordinary months, passed: 375 jobs filled, supplied utilities, no garbage backlog, no loans or emergency, and exact save restoration. At month 1433 the city had 90.274 EQ and §41,281,634. The complete repeatable optional `test:science-city` run from the committed month-749 fixture also passed end to end, reproducing the staged result exactly: offer at month 1421, then month 1433 with 204,544 residents, 90.27355129712825 EQ and §41,281,634. The route uses ordinary policy, construction and historical-designation APIs; no eligibility, education, population or treasury fields are forced.

Browser access is available again. These three cards now describe their own operating effects. Browser recheck verified all three corrected descriptions. All four Science Center preview rotations were visually inspected. ## Large-city routing check

Profiling the month-965 city measured approximately 6.0 seconds for eight full recalculations, with commute routing the largest sampled contributor. A prototype that precomputed edge geometry preserved results but had no consistent benefit and was not adopted. A second prototype accumulates road/highway loads in a Float64Array during route allocation and publishes the same tile fields before reporting. It retains the same allocation, path search, arithmetic order and returned statistics.

Eight alternating warmed samples on the large-city checkpoint measured median routing time of 399.7 ms before and 337.4 ms with the load buffer (about 16% lower). The compared route statistics, tile traffic, commute lengths, commuters, unemployment, civic employment and bus riders matched exactly. This is a local Node benchmark with another simulation running, not a browser or whole-game speed claim. The load-buffer change is included in the pending milestone and all 300 regular regression suites passed again with this change.


The offer cards use an explicit effects catalog whose keys must cover every reward. This prevents newly added rewards from silently receiving University guidance.
