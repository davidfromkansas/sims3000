# Residential health exposure

Manual page 112 says both air and water pollution can reduce life expectancy even with adequate hospitals. Previously the health model deducted only citywide air pollution, including industrial areas without residents. Health now uses population-weighted residential air and water exposure, alongside accessible hospital coverage and recreation. Changes remain gradual; the 45–90-year bounds are preserved. Pollution coefficients are recreation tuning, not values supplied by the manual.

Civic services includes an expandable explanation of current target and each contributing factor. Feedback: compare polluted residential neighborhoods with cleaner homes, maintain hospital funding, then watch life expectancy over several years.

Validation covers population weighting, water pollution, empty land exclusion, hospital benefit, gradual change, bounds, empty cities and reconstruction from saves.
